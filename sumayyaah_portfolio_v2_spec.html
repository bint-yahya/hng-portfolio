01 · WCAG 2.1 AA + DESIGN TOKENS

Colour System & Accessibility

All colours below have been verified to meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast requirements. The accent colour #D4884A is the WCAG-safe version of the original copper — it passes 4.5:1 on dark backgrounds for normal-sized text. The violet #A78BFA similarly passes on dark. Every text/background pairing used in the build must be checked before shipping.

⚠ CRITICAL WCAG REQUIREMENTS — APPLY SITE-WIDE

  • Normal text (under 18px / 14px bold): minimum contrast ratio 4.5:1
  • Large text (18px+ regular or 14px+ bold): minimum contrast ratio 3:1
  • UI components and focus indicators: minimum 3:1
  • The muted text colour #9A9AAC on #09090E achieves ~5.2:1 — acceptable. Never go darker/lower than this for body-size text.
  • Card borders must be visible enough to distinguish cards from background — use rgba(212,136,74,0.25) minimum, or add a 1px solid #2A2A38 fallback.
  • All interactive elements must have a visible focus ring: outline: 2px solid #D4884A; outline-offset: 3px — never remove focus outlines.
  • Font size minimum for body text: 16px (1rem). Labels/tags minimum 12px.
  • Line height minimum 1.6 for body text. Letter-spacing for mono labels: 0.06em minimum.
  • All images must have meaningful alt text. Decorative images: alt="".
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion — wrap all Framer Motion animations in a check and disable/reduce if true.
  • The dark/light mode toggle must have an accessible label: aria-label="Switch to light mode" / "Switch to dark mode".
  • Body font choice: switch from Playfair Display for body text to Inter exclusively. Playfair Display is for headings/display only — it is a serif display face with poor readability at small sizes.

CSS IMPLEMENTATION

  • Define all tokens as CSS custom properties on :root (dark mode defaults). When <html data-theme="light">, override the tokens. This means every component automatically respects both modes without any conditional logic.
  • Card borders: use border: 1px solid var(--border) where --border is #2A2A38 in dark and #D0CAC0 in light. This fixes the "cards disappearing into background" problem — the border is now a solid, visible colour not a transparent rgba that can fail at certain screen calibrations.
  • Body font: Inter 16px, line-height 1.7. Headings: Playfair Display. Labels/tags: Space Grotesk. Load all three from Google Fonts.
  • Minimum interactive touch target: 44×44px. Apply min-height: 44px; min-width: 44px to all buttons and links.

02 · LIGHT / DARK MODE

Theme Toggle Implementation

Dark is the default. The toggle sits in the navigation bar, to the left of the hamburger menu on mobile, and to the right of the nav links on desktop — not replacing the hamburger, sitting beside it.

TOGGLE COMPONENT SPEC

  • Visual: a small pill toggle — moon icon (🌙) for dark, sun icon (☀️) for light. 44px minimum touch target. The icon changes on switch.
  • Accessible: role="switch", aria-checked="true/false", aria-label="Switch to light mode" (updates dynamically to "Switch to dark mode" when in light mode).
  • On click: toggle data-theme attribute on <html> between "dark" (default) and "light". Store preference in localStorage key "theme". On page load, read from localStorage first, then check prefers-color-scheme as fallback.
  • Transition: all CSS custom properties transition smoothly — add transition: background-color 0.3s ease, color 0.3s ease, border-color 0.3s ease to body and card elements. This gives a smooth, professional theme swap rather than a jarring flash.
  • Position desktop: right side of nav, between nav links and right edge. Do not show "Download CV" button — it has been removed from the nav.
  • Position mobile: inside the hamburger overlay menu, at the top of the overlay — a clearly labelled row "Dark Mode / Light Mode" with the toggle.

OatLabs: The Boring Product Campaign

OATLABS CASE STUDY — FULL CONTENT

/projects/oatlabs-boring-product-campaign

01 · THE BRIEF (Business Challenge)

  • Stage 2 of the HNG Marketing Fellowship: select a low-interest, everyday product and build a complete strategy to grow it from $0 to $10K revenue. The task tested the ability to apply core marketing principles to drive growth from scratch — using creativity, structured thinking, and practical execution.
  • I chose oats — not despite the fact that they're boring, but because of it.

02 · MY ROLE & RESPONSIBILITIES

  • Title: Brand Strategist & Campaign Designer
  • Sole creator — all strategy, copy, and creative direction was developed independently
  • Responsibilities: Brand naming and concept development (OatLabs — The Morning Formula™) · Customer persona development (3 personas) · Full-funnel marketing strategy (AIDA) · Ad copywriting (4 variations targeting different audience segments) · Email sequence design (welcome → nurture → conversion) · Growth hack strategy (3 systems) · Landing page prototyping (2 live versions on Netlify) · Revenue roadmap ($0→$10K in 90 days) · Visual ad creative direction

03 · STRATEGY & APPROACH

  • The core insight: Oats have a retention problem. Consumers buy once, eat them plain, get bored, and never come back. The category is large ($9.5B globally) but repeat purchase rate collapses because nobody teaches people how to make them interesting. The "boring" label became the brand's superpower — because the most powerful brand position is one your competitors are too proud to claim.
  • Repositioning: Oats are not a meal — they're a base. OatLabs introduced a modular morning ritual system: Base (premium rolled oats) + Lab (mix-in system: spices, fruits, coffee, dates) + Ritual (a repeatable morning you own). Tagline: "Your daily formula. Your flavour rules."
  • Three customer personas: Temi, 28, The Rushed Professional (Lagos, ₦150k+/mo, Instagram) · Kola, 21, The Campus Optimizer (university, ₦30k budget, TikTok) · Chidi, 33, The Fitness Experimenter (gym 5x/week, tracks macros)
  • Full AIDA funnel: Awareness (TikTok 'Boring Morning' series, paid social) → Interest (recipe carousels, UGC, email newsletter 'The Weekly Formula') → Decision (starter kit landing page, 7-Day Formula Challenge, retargeting) → Action (welcome email, referral programme 'Share a Formula', milestone rewards)

04 · EXECUTION & DELIVERABLES

  • 4 Ad Copies written: Ad 1 targets oat drop-outs ("Oats didn't fail you. Nobody taught you how to use them.") · Ad 2 targets fitness crowd ("Your protein shake has one flavour. Your mornings don't have to.") · Ad 3 scroll-stopper for cold audiences ("They don't want you to know oats taste like this.") · Ad 4 targets busy students/professionals ("4 minutes. 1 bowl. The most underrated breakfast upgrade.")
  • 3 Ad Creatives designed (Canva): "You don't hate oats. You hate boring." (split bowl before/after) · "Same oats. Different life." (Day 3 vs Day 30) · "You've been eating oats wrong." (editorial dark brown style)
  • 3-email sequence: Email 1 (Day 0, welcome — Classic Spice Bowl formula + personalisation question) · Email 2 (Day 5, nurture — Coffee Lab Formula, espresso over oats) · Email 3 (Day 8, conversion — 20% off Starter Kit code LABMEMBER20, 48hr urgency)
  • 3 Growth hacks: Formula Drop Model (limited 72-hour monthly drops with waitlist) · OatLabs Creator Kitchen (customer formula submissions, monthly winner featured and named) · Morning Routine Creator Partnerships (micro-influencers 5k-30k, send Starter Kit, no script)
  • 2 live landing page prototypes (links below in assets section)
  • 10-slide pitch deck presented with revenue roadmap
  • 90-day revenue roadmap: Phase 1 Days 1-30: $0→$1,500 (30 units) · Phase 2 Days 31-60: $1,500→$5,000 (100 units, UGC + gym partnerships) · Phase 3 Days 61-90: $5,000→$10,000 (200 units + 20 subscriptions)

MenuCard: Comprehensive Marketing Strategy

Route: /projects/menucard-comprehensive-strategy · Stage 3 of HNG Marketing Fellowship

MENUCARD COMPREHENSIVE — FULL CONTENT

/projects/menucard-comprehensive-strategy

01 · THE BRIEF (Business Challenge)

  • Stage 3 of the HNG Marketing Fellowship: build a full-scale, multi-channel marketing strategy grounded in real product understanding. Required direct engagement with a startup founder, keyword and market research, and an integrated strategy covering SEO, paid ads, social, content, and email — delivered as a professional presentation.
  • Product: MenuCard — a B2B SaaS product positioned as the operating system restaurants didn't know they needed. It provides QR-code-based digital menus with live inventory sync, real-time order management, sold-out auto-flagging, and analytics. Unlike generic QR menu tools, MenuCard is a control system for the full service shift — before, during, and after service.
  • The problem: Nigerian restaurants aren't losing money to bad menus — they're losing it to operational chaos. Manual order relaying at peak hours, wrong stock counts, zero real-time visibility. ~23% of potential revenue is lost during peak hours due to order errors, stock mismatches, and slow table turnover.

02 · MY ROLE & RESPONSIBILITIES

  • Title: Marketing Strategist
  • Responsibilities: Founder engagement and product deep-dive · Keyword research (Google Keyword Planner, Nigeria-specific) · Competitive landscape analysis · Multi-channel strategy development (SEO, paid ads, social, content, email) · Audience segmentation (B2B and B2C) · Platform content strategy (Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn) · Email funnel architecture (5 distinct audience segments) · Paid ads strategy (Google Search + Meta) · 90-day execution roadmap · 11-slide strategy presentation

03 · STRATEGY & APPROACH

  • The dual-audience insight: MenuCard is a B2B product (restaurant owners buy it) with a B2C surface (diners use it). The social strategy exploited this: content targeting restaurant owners builds the B2B pipeline; content targeting diners builds cultural pressure on restaurants to adopt. Both audiences exist on the same platforms.
  • SEO insight: Keyword Planner data showed low Nigeria-specific search volumes — not because there's no demand, but because the category is still forming. This is exactly where first-mover SEO advantage is built. Strategy: own the problem keywords (restaurant operational pain) before competitors arrive, rather than optimizing for product keywords. "How to manage restaurant orders faster" gets 3× more searches than "digital menu app."
  • Keyword strategy — 3 tiers: Problem-aware (restaurant order management, table turnover speed — high volume, high intent) · Solution-aware (digital menu QR code, contactless ordering app — mid intent) · Brand comparison (GloriaFood alternative, QR menu app Nigeria — high conversion)
  • Positioning: "MenuCard is not a menu tool. MenuCard is the control system that brings order to every shift — before, during, and after service." Ad angle: not 'Get a digital menu' — instead: 'Stop losing orders during your busiest hour.'
  • Two personas: Alex, 35, restaurant manager — loses revenue to order chaos, buys when ROI is obvious. Emily, 28, urban diner — hates wrong orders and slow service, converts when experience is frictionless.

04 · EXECUTION & DELIVERABLES

  • SEO strategy: 3-tier content architecture — Problem Content (awareness, high volume) → Solution Content (consideration, mid-funnel) → Conversion Content (decision, high conversion). Priority keywords documented with monthly search volumes and competition levels.
  • Social media strategy (3 platforms): Instagram (primary — B2B + B2C, Reels as growth engine, carousels as authority, daily Stories, 3-4 Reels/week + 2 carousels) · TikTok (awareness engine — raw, authentic, POV chaos videos, minimum 4-5/week, keyword-optimised captions) · LinkedIn (B2B credibility — data posts, founder voice, case study summaries, 3-4/week)
  • Email funnel (5 audience segments): Cold Leads (educate → free trial desire) · Trial Users (core value in 7 days → conversion) · New Paid Customers (onboarding → prevent Month 1 churn) · Active Customers (retention, feature education, referral) · Churned/Inactive (re-engagement)
  • Paid ads strategy: Google Search Ads (capture high-intent demand — 'restaurant order management system', 'QR menu Nigeria', competitor brand bidding) · Meta/Instagram Ads (create demand — restaurant owners 28-50, urban NG/GH/KE, chaos-to-calm creative angle) · Meta Retargeting (warm audiences)
  • Community building: WhatsApp/Telegram group for Nigerian restaurant owners · Monthly Instagram Live Q&A · UGC system with #PoweredByMenuCard
  • 90-day roadmap: Phase 1 (Days 1-30): SEO infrastructure, 4 cornerstone blog posts, social pages launch, email capture funnel, test Google Search campaigns · Phase 2 (Days 31-60): Meta ads launch, TikTok publishing, full email sequence live, competitor comparison posts · Phase 3 (Days 61-90): retargeting, 3 restaurant case studies, scale winning creative
  • 11-slide strategy deck delivered as polished PDF presentation

05 · RESULTS & OUTCOMES (Projected)

  • First-mover SEO positioning secured across all primary problem-aware and solution-aware keyword clusters before category competition develops in Nigeria
  • Complete multi-channel growth engine designed and ready for execution
  • 5-segment email system covering full customer lifecycle from cold lead to churned user re-engagement
  • Dual-audience social strategy targeting both restaurant owners (B2B acquisition) and diners (market education + social pressure)
  • Note: This was a strategy project delivered as a client presentation — outcomes are strategic projections based on market research and data.

MENUCARD FOUNDER-LED — FULL CONTENT

/projects/menucard-founder-led-strategy
⭐ Scored 10.5 / 10 — Brand owner and mentors cited this as the strongest submission in the cohort

01 · THE BRIEF (Business Challenge)

  • Stage 4B of the HNG Marketing Fellowship: design a founder-led marketing strategy for the same product from Stage 3. Task: position the founder as a compelling personal brand, build visibility across three platforms, drive meaningful engagement, and convert followers into early users — all through the founder's voice, story, and expertise rather than brand advertising.
  • The founder: Michael Abu — certified architect, product designer, and marketer. MenuCard started from a personal frustration: eating out, ordering confidently, and being told half the menu wasn't available. He built the fix instead.
  • The challenge: Most founders position themselves as "startup founders" — crowded, abstract, increasingly distrusted. Michael's architecture background was an underused asset. The strategy needed to turn "certified architect who builds digital products" into a specific, credible, and memorable identity.

02 · MY ROLE & RESPONSIBILITIES

  • Title: Founder Marketing Strategist
  • Responsibilities: Founder narrative and positioning development · Audience segmentation (4 distinct segments) · Content pillar definition (5 pillars) · Tone and voice framework · 4-week multi-platform content calendar (LinkedIn + Instagram + X/Twitter) · Platform-specific content specifications · Engagement and conversion strategy · Community building roadmap · WCAG accessibility considerations for all content

03 · STRATEGY & APPROACH

  • Founder narrative: "I stopped waiting for someone else to fix what was broken and started building the things I wished existed." Michael's arc: builder by training (architecture), builder by instinct (products). RESOLVE — FULL CONTENT

/projects/resolve-website-strategy

01 · THE BRIEF (Business Challenge)

  • Build a website strategy and information architecture for Resolve — an online voting platform helping organizations conduct secure, transparent, and accessible elections. The brief required more than a website brief: it required a full strategic document covering audience analysis, IA, SEO, conversion optimization, WCAG compliance, and a post-launch growth roadmap.
  • The challenge: Resolve's target market (Nigerian organizations — schools, universities, HR teams, NGOs, trade unions, associations) is problem-aware but not solution-aware. Most conduct elections via paper ballots, spreadsheets, or WhatsApp. They understand the frustration of manual elections but don't know dedicated online voting platforms exist. The website had to educate before it could convert.
  • Additional challenge: Trust is the primary conversion factor for an election platform. Organizations are not buying software — they're placing a critical governance process into a third-party platform. Every design and content decision had to reduce perceived risk first.

02 · MY ROLE & RESPONSIBILITIES

  • Title: Website Strategist & Information Architect
  • Responsibilities: Audience analysis and segmentation (primary buyers, secondary influencers, end users) · Information architecture (9-page site map with rationale for every page) · Navigation structure design · User flow mapping (Evaluation Path + Validation Path) · Content tone and voice framework · Conversion optimization strategy (micro, intent, and primary conversions) · CTA architecture (primary, secondary, tertiary) · Lead capture system design · SEO research (Google Keyword Planner, Nigeria — zero measurable volume, interpreted and reframed as first-mover opportunity) · WCAG 2.1 AA compliance checklist (full document) · Post-launch growth strategy (3 phases, 12 months) · Decision matrix for all strategic choices · Website build (Lovable) and deployment

03 · STRATEGY & APPROACH

  • The trust-first insight: "Resolve sells trust before it sells software." The website consistently leads with the user's problem (election stress, transparency concerns, administrative burden) before introducing the product. This reflects how the target audience actually thinks — they're not looking for software, they're looking for a way to run better elections with less stress.
  • Dual CTA architecture: Primary: "Start a Free Election" — removes financial friction, lets organizations experience the product before committing. Secondary: "Request a Demo" — serves organizations with larger requirements or internal approval processes. Both options accommodate different buying patterns without forcing one path.
  • SEO interpretation: Google Keyword Planner returned zero measurable Nigerian search volume for all organizational voting terms. Interpreted as category immaturity — not absence of demand. Strategy: Demand Anticipation SEO — publish authoritative content now to rank when search demand develops as category awareness grows. First-mover advantage in an uncrowded space.
  • Information architecture: Customer journey-based structure (not product category structure) — Awareness (blog) → Consideration (homepage, use cases) → Evaluation (features, pricing) → Decision (free election or demo) → Trust Verification (about, privacy, T&C). Every page has a defined role.
  • Community layer: The Decision Lab — a governance-focused community for HR professionals, association leaders, and organizational decision-makers. Creates long-term relationships with potential customers before they need the product. An audience asset independent of search algorithms or ad costs.
  • WCAG approach: Full WCAG 2.1 AA compliance checklist developed and applied — not as a box-ticking exercise but as a reflection of Resolve's mission (inclusive, transparent elections). The platform promoting accessible voting must itself be accessible.

04 · EXECUTION & DELIVERABLES
